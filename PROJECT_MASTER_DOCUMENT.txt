2. PROJECT PURPOSE AND PROBLEM STATEMENT
================================================================================
2.1 The Real-World Problem
Amateur, collegiate, and regional sports organizers face fragmented, manual tools:
1. Tournament logistics (draws, brackets, team registrations, fee collection, approvals) are typically tracked across WhatsApp groups and Google Sheets.
2. Live scoring is disconnected from fan viewing: local tournaments lack automated live scoreboards, ball-by-ball tracking, or interactive visual bracket trees.
3. Athletes have no persistent digital portfolio: player accomplishments, match statistics, videos, reels, and tournament titles vanish after the event finishes.
4. Social engagement around amateur athletics is either dispersed across general social networks (Instagram, Facebook) without sports context or buried in niche forums.

2.2 Proposed Solution (SportSphere)
SportSphere solves this by delivering a unified digital arena:
- Organizers create multi-sport tournaments with customizable formats (single-elimination knockout, league, round-robin), automated bracket scheduling, and participant management.
- Captains create teams, manage player rosters, and register for regional tournaments.
- Scorers operate interactive, sport-specific consoles (cricket ball-by-ball, badminton/volleyball/tennis points, football goals) that broadcast real-time score updates to fans via WebSockets.
- Athletes & Fans share multimedia highlight reels, follow players/teams, join dedicated tournament and team live chat rooms, and build a verified sports resume.

3. HOW THE COMPLETE SYSTEM WORKS (END-TO-END WORKFLOW)
================================================================================
Step 1: Onboarding & Authentication
A user registers via email/password or Google OAuth. They select an identity role: 'player', 'organizer', or 'fan'. Upon registration, a User record and corresponding Profile document with social preferences and statistics are provisioned. A welcome email is triggered asynchronously.

Step 2: Tournament Creation & Publishing
An Organizer navigates to `/tournaments/create` and inputs tournament parameters: Sport type (Cricket, Football, Badminton, Volleyball, Kabaddi, etc.), format (Single-elimination knockout, League, etc.), team constraints (min/max teams), venues with geographic coordinates, registration dates, fees, and rules. Once saved, the tournament appears in the public `/tournaments` directory and interactive Leaflet map `/tournaments#map`.

Step 3: Team Formation & Tournament Registration
A Team Captain builds a team under `/teams/create`, selects a sport, uploads a team crest, and invites athletes by user search or email. The Captain browses tournaments, selects an open tournament, and registers their squad. The organizer reviews pending registrations in `/tournaments/:id/participants` and approves or rejects entries.

Step 4: Automated Bracket Draw & Scheduling
When registrations close, the Organizer launches the tournament via `/tournaments/:id/brackets`. The backend validates the team count, generates tournament matchups (Round of 16, Quarter-Finals, Semi-Finals, Final), creates persistent `Match` records in MongoDB, and constructs the bracket tree.

Step 5: Live Match Operations & Real-Time Scoring
During match day, officials open `/tournaments/:id/matches/:matchId/score` (or `/tournaments/:id/scoring`). Scorer actions (cricket ball updates with runs/wickets/extras, or badminton/volleyball set points) trigger REST updates that persist to MongoDB and broadcast WebSocket payloads (`match-score-updated`, `bracket-updated`) to all viewers connected to `/tournaments/:id/live`.

Step 6: Fan Experience & Community Engagement
Spectators watch live scores, examine the bracket progression, view standings, and interact in the real-time tournament chat room. Players capture photos and videos and publish them to the community feed at `/posts`. Users like, comment, follow other athletes, and earn gamified profile badges.

9. MISSING FEATURES
================================================================================

   - Registration fees (`registrationFee`) and prize pools are defined in models, but no payment gateway (Razorpay, Stripe, or PayPal) is implemented. All payments are simulated status flags (`pending`, `paid`).

2. Standalone Match Creation & Match Directory:
   - The `/matches` and `/matches/create` pages exist, but `GET /api/matches` currently has 0 matches. Standalone friendly/scrimmage matches outside of tournaments cannot be scheduled or completed through a validated end-to-end flow.

3. Automated Tournament Match Progression (Winner Advancement):
   - When a match score is entered in `ScoreEntry.js`, the winner is recorded, but the system does not automatically advance the winning team into the subsequent round match slot in the tournament bracket.

4. Email Password Reset Delivery:
   - In `server/src/controllers/auth.js` (`forgotPassword`), the `sendEmail` call is commented out. The server returns the raw token in the response JSON instead of emailing a secure reset link.

5. Content Reporting & Flagging Queue:
   - Users can report posts via `POST /api/posts/:id/report`, but the Admin Content Moderation page (`ContentModeration.js`) loads all posts indiscriminately rather than filtering for reported items (`reports.length > 0`).

11.1 Executive Summary
SportSphere is a web-based, multi-sport tournament management and social engagement platform. It bridges the gap between grassroots athletic competition and modern social networking by giving amateur leagues professional-grade bracket automation, live real-time scorekeeping, team management, and athlete portfolios.

11.2 Product Vision and Mission
- Vision: To become the premier digital operating system for amateur and community sports worldwide.
- Mission: Empower local sports organizers with effortless event management, provide athletes with persistent recognition, and give fans an interactive, real-time window into local sporting events.

11.3 Problem Statement
Community sports tournaments suffer from manual scheduling, fragmented communication, lack of transparent live scoring, and absent digital records for players. Organizers struggle with logistics, while athletes have no unified platform to showcase their progress and highlights.

11.4 Project Purpose and Objectives
1. Reduce tournament setup and bracket creation time from hours to under 2 minutes.
2. Deliver sub-second real-time score updates to spectators via WebSockets.
3. Foster athlete engagement through multimedia sharing, follow networks, and automated skill stats.
4. Provide tournament organizers with clear participant approval, communication, and scheduling tools.

11.5 Target Audience and User Personas
- Persona 1: Rajesh, 34, Regional Cricket League Organizer. Needs to register 16 local teams, generate a knockout draw, assign venues across Bhimavaram, and provide live ball-by-ball updates to families at home.

- Persona 3: Marcus, 27, Fitness Enthusiast & Football Player. Uses the platform to share match highlights, discover nearby weekend tournaments on an interactive map, and track workouts.

11.6 User Problems and Proposed Solutions
- Problem: Brackets drawn on paper or static graphics require manual recreation whenever teams change.
  Solution: Dynamic single-elimination / round-robin bracket generator with automated database-backed match fixtures.
- Problem: Spectators cannot attend every local game and have no way to track scores live.
  Solution: WebSocket-powered live match board broadcasting instant ball-by-ball and point-by-point updates.
- Problem: Athletes lose track of their personal achievements and game statistics.
  Solution: Dedicated Profile model with auto-calculated win rates, career stats, and earned achievement badges.

The scope encompasses multi-sport tournament creation, team administration, automated fixture generation, live scoring consoles, real-time messaging/chat, user social feeds (posts, likes, comments), athlete profiles, and administrative oversight.
